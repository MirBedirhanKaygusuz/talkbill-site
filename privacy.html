Privacy Policy

Talkbill · Last updated 16 August 2026

We collect nothing. Talkbill has no account system and no server. There is no database of ours that your invoices could sit in, because there is no database of ours.

What happens to your voice

When you describe a job, the audio is transcribed on your iPhone using Apple's on-device speech recognition. The recording is not saved and the audio never leaves your device. Talkbill does not upload audio or transcripts anywhere, and does not use any cloud speech service.

Where your invoices live

Your customers, invoices and business details are stored on your iPhone. If you are signed in to iCloud, iOS mirrors them to your own private iCloud database so they survive a lost or replaced phone. That storage belongs to your Apple account — we have no access to it, and we cannot read, export or delete anything in it. You can turn iCloud off in iOS Settings; Talkbill keeps working entirely on the device.

Third parties

Talkbill contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK and no third-party tracking of any kind. Purchases are handled by Apple's App Store through StoreKit; Apple tells the app only whether a subscription is active. We never see your payment details, and no purchase information is linked to your identity by us.

Permissions

Microphone — required, because speaking is how you make an invoice. Used only while you are actively dictating. Photos — optional, only if you pick a logo for your invoices; the image is stored with your business profile on your device.

Your data is never held hostage

If your subscription lapses, your invoices remain readable and exportable — as CSV and PDF — for as long as the app is installed. Ending a subscription locks new voice invoices, never your existing records. Deleting the app removes the local data; anything mirrored to iCloud is removed through your Apple account.

Children

Talkbill is a tool for running a business and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone — of any age — because we do not collect information at all.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated date above changes with it. Because the app collects nothing, any change here would mean a change in the app itself — and that would be described in the App Store release notes too.
